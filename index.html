The evening, course by course

Four courses to a great impression

Before: plan the eveningSource 1Bennett (2011),Source 11Missouri State Career Center (n.d.)

  • Reserve a place you already know: easy to reach, quiet, and midrange in price.
  • Ask about your guest’s tastes and allergies first.
  • Arrive at least 15 minutes early. US

US marks a United States custom.

Find a midrange restaurant on OpenTable (opens in a new tab)

Ordering: keep it simpleSource 1Bennett (2011),Source 3Dizik (2011),Source 12UC Riverside Career Center (n.d.)

  • Hosting? Let your guest order first, then match their price level.
  • Pick dishes that are easy to eat. Skip spaghetti, ribs, and lobster.
  • Unsure about wine? Ask for a midrange recommendation.

At the table: connect firstSource 3Dizik (2011),Source 13UNL Business Career Center (n.d.)

  • Silence your phone and keep it out of sight.
  • Open with small talk. Bring up business partway through the meal. US
  • Listen at least as much as you talk.

After: a gracious finishSource 3Dizik (2011),Source 12UC Riverside Career Center (n.d.)

  • Whoever invites pays. If you were invited, don’t fight for the bill.
  • Thank your host at the table, then send a quick email, call, or card.
  • End on a relaxed, friendly note.

Etiquette around the worldSource 6International Trade Administration (2024),Source 7International Trade Administration (2025),Source 8International Trade Administration (2026)

This guide follows US business customs. Abroad, adapt:

  • Spain Lunch starts around 2 p.m. and can run two hours. Tipping is optional.
  • Brazil Be on time, but take delays calmly.
  • France Politeness and a touch of formality matter most.
